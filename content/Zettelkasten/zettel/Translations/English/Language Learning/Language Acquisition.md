## Claim

Language acquisition is the construction of an inner language system that connects perception, meaning, and use. You do not acquire only individual words or rules, but a system in which sound, lexis, semantics, grammar, and pragmatics become available together in real situations.[^clipping-what-is-language-acquisition]

> [!NOTE]
> > Language acquisition is the natural, subconscious process by which humans gain the ability to perceive, produce, and use words to communicate, typically starting from birth. It differs from conscious "language learning" by focusing on functional fluency through exposure, rather than explicit rule memorization.[^definition-source]

The goal is broader than "words plus grammar." What is acquired is a connected system of sound, vocabulary, boundaries of meaning, grammatical forms, and pragmatic use.[^clipping-what-is-language-acquisition]

## Why It Matters

For language learning, this shifts the focus: input must not merely be abundant; it must **create acquisition conditions**. It should be comprehensible enough, repeated enough, and relevant enough for patterns to become noticeable and connect into a usable feel for the language.[^input-output-immersion-sources]

> [!QUESTION]
> What exactly does it mean, in practice, to "create acquisition conditions," and how can you tell whether input is actually doing that?

Immersion is powerful because it carries these layers at the same time: a real sentence brings together sound, meaning, grammar, situation, and social function.[^input-output-immersion-sources]
