# Immersion Turns Culture Into the Environment

## Claim

Immersion means treating a language not merely as study material, but as an environment. You move inside a medium of communication, a culture, and an information network in which meaning, sound, register, typical situations, and world knowledge appear together.[^source-notes]

In practice, this means moving as much as possible inside the target language's information network. You read, listen, search, react, half-understand, understand better later, and learn which expressions carry which situations.

The important point is the monolingual nature of the experience: the target language should not constantly be explained through translation. It should itself be the medium in which you understand, search, fail, correct yourself, and continue reading or listening.

You always learn the culture along with the language. A language is not merely a stock of vocabulary, but a network of conventions: idiom, register, typical topics, social expectations, media forms, and recurring formulations. Words, phrases, and typical ways of saying things are tied to the situations, roles, politeness patterns, gestures, and expectations that are normal in a speech community.

## Why It Matters

A feel for the language does not grow only from rules and vocabulary. It grows from repeated orientation inside real contexts of meaning. Progress is not only the accumulation of individual new words, but increasingly efficient movement through the whole communication space.
