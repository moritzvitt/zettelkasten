# A Language Is Learned Deeply Once and Reactivated Later

## Claim

A language does not have to be learned from scratch every time. Once learned deeply enough, many patterns remain in the brain: sound, sentence rhythm, typical forms, semantic fields, routines, and cultural expectations.[^source-notes]

What is often lost first is accessibility. Fluency may not return immediately, but after several days of dense re-exposure the language can switch back on. Learning and reactivation are therefore different tasks.

Attrition research supports this distinction: in late bilinguals, some effects may depend more on activation state, language mode, environment, and processing competition than on erased representations. Re-exposure can therefore restore performance relatively quickly.

## Why It Matters

Language learning becomes less fragile. The goal is to establish a robust level once, then later restore access through input, output, and targeted support. SRS may keep access paths open, but cannot replace reactivation through real contact.

[^source-notes]: Source notes: Mehrsprachigkeit als Reaktivierung; First Language Attrition and Dominance Same Same or Different; Japanisch und Anki Backfill.
