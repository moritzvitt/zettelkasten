# Nexus Compares Democracy and Dictatorship as Information Networks

## Claim

*Nexus* reads democracy and dictatorship as different information architectures. Dictatorships centralize information and decision-making; democracies distribute information, criticism, and correction across many nodes.

The advantage of democratic networks is not that they are always wiser. Their advantage lies in institutionally expecting error and therefore being able to build in ways to check power and correct mistakes.

## Why It Matters

Democracy becomes weak when its correction mechanisms are destroyed: the press, courts, science, opposition, and free public debate. Election rhetoric may remain, but the information network moves closer to centralized power.
