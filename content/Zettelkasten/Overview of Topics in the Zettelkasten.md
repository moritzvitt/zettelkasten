# Why should you learn a language, and what is it even that you are learning?

![[zettelkasten/zettel/immersion-and-language-acquisition/language-learning.excalidraw#^frame=What is a Language?|100%]]

The first question we ask usually is "Why" (...should you do something. In our case: Why should you learn a language). In our case however, the "Why" and the "What" (What is a language? What are we actually learning when we say "I am learning a language") are so closely tied to each other, that we can't answer one first before tackling the other question. 

## We need to communicate

We humans are social beings. Meaning, we communicate with each other (and we need to do so). 
A baby, that gets ignored, dies, and a person without social contact gets depressed. 

All beings communicate probably to some extent , but we do so excessively. 

## Learning how to exchange information (becoming a node in an information network)

Now, [[zettelkasten/zettel/communication-and-information/communication|communication]] per definition is the exchange of information. 
Over the years, we humans came up with many different ways of doing so. We call these "information technology".

## What is information?

Now information is a slippery thing. It's hard to pin down. 

Many common assumptions about information don't quite get it. 

information is a fact! - wrong, information can be false
information is an appeal to do something - wrong 

All these are close, but not yet there. 
The real (value/purpose, welches wort kann ich hier verwenden) lies in its connectivity (moritz: ist connectivity das richtige Wort?)

Information connects things in an information network! 

Imagine the following scenario:

You are walking down the street and see your best friend on the other side of it. 
You wave your hand, he sees you, and comes over to greet you back. 

What happened? 
You sent out an information, and your friend, upon receiving, altered his behaviour (if it weren't for you, he would have continued his way down the road.) 

Now, you two build a network. 


Imagine the following: 
You are hunting in the Savanna with your best friend. 
You can hear a lion close by. You climb on the highest rock in sight and can now see over the Savanna. You can see that the lion is far away. You are relieved. You climb down from the rock and tell your friend. 

Now.

## Information Technology

First, let's clarify that a spoken word itself doesn't carry any meaning. It's just a string of sounds. The meaning is created in my brain, upon hearing those sounds. 


The primary information technologies we use we communicate through **language** (spoken or written) and **body language** (behaviour)

Furthermore, since the rise of mathematics and statistics, we learned to **communicate through numbers**.

To do so, we use a range of information (communication) technology.

The ways in which we communicate differ from culture to culture:

Some behaviour, like smiling, is universal to all cultures. 
Other behaviour, for example looking somebody in the eyes when talking for example (Germany: respect, Senegal: disrespect) carry different meaning in different cultures. 

Speaking a language is understanding the people. 



A [[zettelkasten/zettel/communication-and-information/language|Language]], such as English or Japanese, per definition is merely a means of [[zettelkasten/zettel/communication-and-information/communication|communication]] (spoken or written).  (moritz: korrigiere die Großschreibung der Sprachen im letzten Satz, falls sie falsch sein sollte.)

In a culture however exists several other means of [[zettelkasten/zettel/communication-and-information/means-of-communication-within-a-culture|Means of Communication Within a Culture]] [[zettelkasten/zettel/communication-and-information/culture-dependent-means-of-communication|Culture-Dependent Means of Communication]]
However, when we are learning a language, we usually intuitively acquire typical behaviour typical such as body language (mimic and gestures) or rituals (e.g. greetings, forms of politeness). 

In the end, learning a language is more about, becoming part of, or assimilating into, a culture. 
It means: to learn how to be in that culture, or in more technical terms: to learn how to transmit information (effectively) in that culture [[zettelkasten/zettel/communication-and-information/nexus-and-information/culture-as-an-information-network|Culture as an Information Network]].
