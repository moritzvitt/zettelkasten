# **The Why and the What**

## **What do we mean when we say “I am learning a language”?**

Before talking about vocabulary, grammar or Anki, it is worth asking a more fundamental question:

**What does it actually mean to learn a language?**

And perhaps an even more important one:

**Why do humans learn languages in the first place?**

---

## **We need to communicate** (to cooperate)

We humans are social beings. Meaning, we communicate with each other (excessively, compared to other species).

And we need to do so—a baby that gets ignored dies, and a person without social contact eventually becomes depressed.

[[zettelkasten/zettel/communication-and-information/communication|communication]] is not merely something we enjoy. It is fundamental to human life. We are meant to communicate. 

---

## **Communication allows us to organise ourselves**

Imagine the following scenario:

You see your friend down the road. You wave.
He sees you and comes over to shake your hand. You have a chat.
Then your friend tells you that he is free this afternoon and you decide to go to a café together.

Originally, both of you had other plans.

Now you will spend the afternoon together.

**What caused this change?**

You exchanged information.

Through communication, your behaviour became synchronised.
This is what communication does—not only between two friends, but throughout society.

Every day we exchange information about weather, politics, sports, science, art, family, work and countless other things.

In doing so, we constantly coordinate our behaviour.

---

## [[zettelkasten/zettel/communication-and-information/nexus-and-information/culture-as-an-information-network|Culture is an information network]]

A culture exists because people continuously exchange information.
This leads to a unique way of organisation of a group of people. 

Culture is, what we call the bundle of methods a group of people uses to organise themselves. 

The communication, shared knowledge, traditions, [[zettelkasten/zettel/communication-and-information/institutions|Institutions]] and common understanding of the world they use to do that. 

A culture can be understood an information network: connected through continuous communication.

---

## **Learning a language means joining that network**

This clarifies the real goal of language learning.

==Learning a language is learning how to connect with people from another culture through communication.== 
To achieve that, you need to become a node in that information network yourself.

That means learning not only the language itself, but also the other ways in which people exchange information, institutions:

- spoken language
- written language
- body language (Some body language, like smiling, is nearly universal to all cultures but it can still mean different things.)
- humour
- politeness
- shared cultural references

[[zettelkasten/zettel/communication-and-information/institutions-of-a-culture|institutions of the culture]]:
- greetings (are usually ritualised)
- common situations (rituals, traditions)
- social nors
- conversational habits
